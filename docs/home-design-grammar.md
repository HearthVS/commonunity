# CommonUnity hOMe Design Grammar

## 1. Thesis

A hOMe is the outward-facing public doorway of a person's Minimum Viable Digital Self: the smallest, least artificial digital expression that lets a visitor encounter the real person, built on a hidden CommonUnity meaning-and-source engine that never surfaces its own internal language, taxonomy, or process on the public-facing page.

---

## 2. What hOMe Is / Is Not

**hOMe is:**
- A public, shareable personal doorway — the thing you'd put in a bio link or send to a stranger.
- Person-first: the visitor meets a human being, not a system or a methodology.
- Familiar enough to work on the open web — legible in one glance, navigable without instructions.
- More honest, coherent, and alive than a typical Web2 personal site, portfolio, or landing page, without being confusing or novel for novelty's sake.
- A *holding*, not a container: it holds the person's expression; it does not contain, define, or replace them.
- Built on top of CommonUnity's internal architecture, but that architecture is invisible scaffolding — visible in stUdio, absent in hOMe.

**hOMe is not:**
- A visualization of the Compass, Living Profile, Nexus, or any other CommonUnity internal system.
- A place where framework language, provenance strips, or "source" metadata appear as public UI.
- A mystical, cryptic, or intentionally disorienting threshold experience.
- A fixed taxonomy the visitor must learn (e.g., "Work / Lens / Field / Call" as literal on-screen labels).
- A replacement for the person — it is the least artificial digital expression of them, not a digital twin or a complete self.

---

## 3. Public vs. Internal Language Rules

**Rule of thumb:** if a word describes CommonUnity's own process or architecture, it stays behind the builder wall. If a word describes something the visitor can plainly see and understand about the person, it can go public.

**Never expose publicly, as fixed labels, chrome, or UI copy:**
- Compass
- Living Profile
- Nexus
- Field Observations
- Spark
- "Work / Lens / Field / Call" as literal, fixed section names
- Provenance strips, source tags, confidence scores, or any "this came from X system" metadata
- Any explicit reference to CommonUnity's internal framework, methodology, or engine

**Always true of public-facing language:**
- It reads like something a thoughtful person would title their own site section, not like software navigation.
- It is customizable per person — CommonUnity does not impose one universal vocabulary.
- It can still be warm, plain, and confident — this is not about euphemism, it's about ownership. The person's language, not the platform's.

**Where internal language is allowed:**
- stUdio (the builder/editor surface) — where the person is actively constructing their hOMe, and can see the underlying dimensions, prompts, and system logic.
- Internal CommonUnity documentation, specs, and team communication (like this document).

**The engine/visitor split:** CommonUnity owns the hidden meaning-and-source engine — the logic that organizes someone's inputs into coherent dimensions. The visitor never sees the engine. The visitor experiences the person.

## 4. Minimum Viable Digital Self — Principles

Every hOMe should be built and evaluated against two standing questions:

- **"What is my Minimum Viable Digital Self?"** — the smallest, truest digital expression that still lets this person be encountered honestly.
- **"How digital can I become?"** — an open, ongoing question, not a mandate to maximize digital presence.

**Governing rule:** Digitize only what deepens **coherence, expression, service, and liberation.** If a feature, field, or flourish doesn't serve one of these four, it doesn't belong in the hOMe — no matter how easy it is to build or how common it is on Web2 platforms.

**What this rules out:**
- Vanity metrics, engagement bait, infinite feeds, algorithmic performance pressure.
- Over-collection of personal data "just in case" it becomes useful.
- Feature parity with Web2 portfolio sites for its own sake (testimonials carousels, stock CTA buttons, generic "About Me" boilerplate) unless it genuinely serves the person's coherence or invitation.

**What this must not flatten:**
- Offers, services, invitations, and projects should not become lifeless just because the builder is avoiding Web2 boilerplate. The goal is not to suppress selling, invitation, proof, or momentum; the goal is to make them feel honest, specific, alive, and coherent with the person.
- A hOMe should help the person's real offerings become more vivid, not less. If a service, product, practice, course, session, community, creative work, or collaboration path is genuinely part of the person's expression, the builder should give it form, hierarchy, and energy.
- "Not generic Web2" does not mean "anti-conversion," "anti-offer," or "anti-spark." The public hOMe still needs clear invitations, next steps, and moments of desire. They should feel like natural openings in the person's digital home, not stock CTA modules pasted onto a template.

**What this affirms:**
- A hOMe can be sparse. Sparse is not unfinished — it can be the honest answer to "minimum viable."
- A hOMe grows as the person's self-understanding grows, not as a checklist of profile completeness.

## 5. Four Hidden Dimensions & Public Label Personalization

The four dimensions are **builder/source architecture** — the organizing structure CommonUnity uses to make sense of a person's inputs. They are not a public taxonomy and should never appear as fixed, universal on-screen labels.

| # | Internal dimension | What it captures | Public framing guidance |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | **What I do** | Craft, work, offerings, creations | Often the most Web2-legible dimension. Public label can be as plain as "Work," "What I make," "My craft," or a specific title relevant to the person's field. |
| 2 | **How I perceive** | Whole-person perception: sensing, pattern recognition, learning, meaning-making — not just visual "how I see." Must acknowledge the limits of a screen-based medium to represent whole-person perception. | Resist collapsing this into a gallery or "vision" moodboard. Public label should point at how the person understands the world — could be "How I notice things," "Ways I think," "Perspective," or omitted if not yet coherent enough to share. |
| 3 | **What sustains me** | Practices, vitality, relationships, places, rhythms, community, service | Public label might be "What keeps me going," "Roots," "Rhythms," or specific practices named by the person. |
| 4 | **What calls me** | Purpose, mission, service, invitation | Often the closing/invitation section of a hOMe. Public label might be "What I'm here for," "Where I'm headed," or a direct call to action phrased personally. |

**Personalization rules:**
- Every visible label is user-editable free text, seeded by (but not locked to) the dimension's meaning.
- The four dimensions do not have to map 1:1 to four visible sections — a person may merge, reorder, hide, or emphasize dimensions unevenly. The hidden architecture stays four-part for CommonUnity's own coherence-building; the public surface is not obligated to mirror that count or order.
- No dimension should default to its internal name if the person hasn't set a custom label. Default public copy should be human and inviting, never the taxonomy term itself.

## 6. Visitor Experience Grammar

Lessons from Prototype 2, generalized into standing grammar:

- **Person-first hero.** The first thing a visitor sees is the person — name, presence, a human entry point — not a process gate, system explanation, or "begin your journey" threshold. Process-first thresholds tested worse and should be avoided by default.
- **Warm, familiar public voice.** Public copy uses plain, warm, confident language. Dark, mystical, or internal-process tonality (even when well-intentioned as "depth") reads as alienating on the open web and should be reserved, if at all, for stUdio's internal builder framing — not the public hOMe.
- **Conventional-enough navigation.** Outsiders should be able to orient within seconds using patterns they already know (clear top-level sections, obvious scroll or click affordances, visible way to go "back" or "home"). Do not require a visitor to learn a new interaction language to read someone's page.
- **Field-like or room-like navigation is permitted, not disorienting.** Spatial or exploratory metaphors (moving between "rooms" or areas of a person's world) can coexist with conventional wayfinding — but disorientation is a bug, not a feature. If a visitor can't tell where they are or how to get back, the pattern has failed regardless of how evocative it is.
- **No provenance exposure.** Visitors never see labels like "sourced from Field Observations" or confidence/origin metadata. If something needs attribution, it's phrased in human terms the person chose, not system terms.
- **Coherent invitation, not confusion-as-depth.** The differentiation from Web2 comes from honesty and clarity, not from making the visitor work to understand what they're looking at. Novelty in service of truthful self-expression is welcome; novelty in service of seeming sophisticated is not.

## 7. Visual / Interaction Grammar

- **Baseline legibility over experimental chrome.** Typography, spacing, and layout should read as competent and calm — closer to a well-made personal site than an app dashboard or a generative-art experiment.
- **Restraint as a feature.** Motion, transitions, and spatial navigation should support orientation, not perform complexity. Prototype 2's success came from de-escalating mystique in favor of warmth — visual grammar should follow the same instinct.
- **Consistent identity anchor.** A visitor should always be able to tell whose hOMe they're in and have a clear, persistent way back to a "home" state within the page, even if navigation is spatial/room-like.
- **No system chrome bleed-through.** UI elements that belong to CommonUnity's builder (dimension names, source tags, edit affordances, framework labels) must not leak into the visitor-facing render, including in empty/placeholder states.
- **Graceful sparseness.** Visual design must hold up when a person has only filled in one or two dimensions. A minimal hOMe should look intentional, not broken or "under construction."
- **Strong visual identity is required, not optional.** A hOMe cannot be a text document with polite spacing. Each public hOMe needs a visual language that gives the person's presence form: color, type, marks, atmosphere, rhythm, imagery, motion, and negative space should work together as an expression of the person.
- **Visuals carry selfhood, not decoration.** Graphics, color, and imagery should not be added as generic ornament. They should translate something real: the person's tempo, density, warmth, edge, clarity, craft, place, practices, sensory world, and invitation. When the source material is thin, the builder should choose restraint; when the source material is rich, the visual system should become correspondingly more alive.
- **Color is personal signal.** Do not default all hOMes to a single CommonUnity palette. CommonUnity may provide safe palette roles and accessibility constraints, but the actual palette should be derived from the person's source material, preferences, imagery, OM/Compass-derived cues where appropriate internally, and the emotional tone of the public hOMe. The public visitor should experience the person's atmosphere, not the platform's brand.
- **OM Cipher is a baseline input, not public branding.** The builder should treat the person's OM Cipher palette as a first-class internal color source: a baseline energetic/personal palette that can seed public color direction, accents, atmosphere, and contrast choices. The public hOMe should not need to mention OM Cipher, but the visual generator should know when it is using, adapting, softening, or overriding those colors for legibility, taste, and fit.
- **Imagery should be meaningful and consentful.** A hOMe can use portraits, places, objects, textures, generated atmospheres, drawings, symbols, or abstract fields — but every image should answer "what does this help someone encounter about this person?" Avoid stock imagery, decorative icons, and empty visual slots. If no strong image exists yet, generate or compose a visual treatment that feels intentional rather than placeholder.
- **Minimum viable does not mean visually minimal.** A minimum viable digital self may be sparse in content while still rich in feeling. The design system must support both quiet editorial hOMes and visually expressive hOMes without forcing either extreme.
- **The screen is a limitation to work with.** Because perception is embodied and multisensory, the visual layer should hint beyond the purely visual when possible: rhythm, pacing, texture, sound-ready structure, environmental cues, and writing cadence can help express perception without pretending the screen contains the whole person.

## 8. Builder Implications (stUdio)

This section translates the public-facing grammar into requirements for the stUdio builder and underlying data model.

- **Data model outputs:**
  - Each of the four dimensions stores both (a) the person's raw/source inputs and (b) a derived public-facing block with its own editable label, body content, and visibility toggle.
  - Public label field is always free text, defaulted to a friendly placeholder — never the raw dimension name.
  - A dimension can be empty, hidden, merged with another, or reordered without breaking the page layout.

- **Required internal source inputs:**
  - The hOMe builder should ingest the person's cOMpass JSON as a primary content seed. This file already carries initial structured material that can populate identity, threshold, dimension copy, highlights, themes, and early offers before additional stUdio refinement.
  - The builder should ingest the OM Cipher / palette layer as a primary visual seed. It should map baseline personal colors into public-facing palette roles rather than treating color as an afterthought or generic theme choice.
  - These inputs are internal builder materials. They should shape the public hOMe deeply, but their system names should not appear on the public hOMe.

- **Source mapping:**
  - Internal source/provenance metadata (what fed into a given block — e.g., which Field Observation or Spark) is retained in stUdio for the person's own editing context, but is a builder-only property. It must have no rendering path into the public hOMe view, including via tooltips, alt text, hidden DOM attributes, or debug states.
  - stUdio can and should show the person the internal framework (Compass, dimensions, etc.) since this is where the "How digital can I become?" reflection happens — that transparency is appropriate and valuable in the builder, not in public.

- **Preview behavior:**
  - stUdio should implement a **preview overlay pattern**: from the work surface, the person opens a visitor preview (rendered exactly as a stranger would see it) as an overlay, then closes it to return to the same point on the work surface.
  - Preview must be a true render of public output — no internal labels, no builder chrome, no edit affordances — so the person can validate "how digital do I want to become" before publishing.
  - Preview should be quick to invoke and dismiss, encouraging frequent checks rather than a rare, heavyweight "publish and see" cycle.

- **Publish readiness:**
  - A hOMe should be publishable with as little as one populated dimension — publish readiness is not gated on taxonomy completeness, consistent with Minimum Viable Digital Self principles.
  - Before publish, run an automated check that no internal terms (Compass, Living Profile, Nexus, Field Observations, Spark, literal "Work/Lens/Field/Call" labels, provenance strings) appear in the rendered public output.
  - Publishing should feel like a deliberate, low-ceremony act consistent with "least artificial expression" — not a heavy launch event.

- **Visual model outputs:**
  - The hOMe model should include public-facing visual direction, not just copy: palette roles, type direction, layout density, image strategy, motion intensity, symbolic/graphic motifs, and optional sensory cues.
  - The builder should generate visual hypotheses from the same source material that generates copy. It should not wait for the user to manually design the site after the meaning engine has finished.
  - Palette generation should begin from the OM Cipher baseline and then translate it into usable public roles: background, surface, text, accent, secondary accent, room/dimension emphasis, focus states, and contrast-safe fallbacks.
  - Visual choices should be editable and explainable in stUdio: "warmer / quieter / more direct / more spacious / more vivid / more grounded" is more useful than exposing raw design tokens.
  - Empty visual states must render intentionally. Never show "visual slot coming," broken placeholders, or generic cards where a person-facing visual system should be.

## 9. Anti-Patterns / Things Not to Do

- Do not render internal taxonomy terms (Compass, Living Profile, Nexus, Field Observations, Spark, fixed "Work/Lens/Field/Call" labels) anywhere on the public hOMe.
- Do not show provenance strips, source tags, or "this content came from..." attributions in visitor-facing UI.
- Do not open a hOMe with a process gate, system explanation, or mystical threshold screen instead of the person.
- Do not adopt dark/mystical internal-process tonality as the default public voice.
- Do not require visitors to learn a novel interaction pattern just to find basic sections of the page.
- Do not force all four dimensions to be filled, visible, or evenly weighted before a hOMe can be published.
- Do not let spatial/room-like navigation ship without a clear, persistent way to reorient or return home.
- Do not treat feature parity with Web2 portfolio templates as a goal in itself; only add what deepens coherence, expression, service, or liberation.
- Do not turn caution about Web2 templates into flatness. Offers, invitations, projects, proof, and calls to engage still need life, hierarchy, and desire when they are true to the person.
- Do not let the hOMe become text-dominated by default. Minimal content can still have a strong visual atmosphere; rich content still needs visual rhythm.
- Do not use stock visuals, empty placeholders, or one-size-fits-all CommonUnity styling as a substitute for person-derived visual language.
- Do not let stUdio's internal builder language or affordances leak into preview or published output.
- Do not treat "more digital" as inherently better — completeness is not the same as coherence.

## 10. Near-Term Implementation Roadmap

1. **Codify the language firewall.** Build the automated pre-publish check that scans rendered hOMe output for banned internal terms and provenance patterns; treat any violation as a publish blocker.
2. **Refactor default labels.** Replace any current fixed dimension-name defaults in the UI with warm, human placeholder copy per dimension, and confirm all labels are editable free text.
3. **Rebuild the hero/opening flow.** Replace process-first threshold openings with a person-first hero pattern across active prototypes, per Prototype 2 findings.
4. **Retune public voice.** Audit existing public copy for dark/mystical/internal-process tonality and rewrite in warm, plain, confident language; keep the richer internal voice reserved for stUdio.
5. **Implement the preview overlay.** Build the stUdio work-surface → visitor-preview overlay → close-back-to-work-surface interaction as a first-class, low-friction feature.
6. **Stress-test sparse hOMes.** Validate that one- and two-dimension hOMes render as intentional and coherent, not broken, across the visual grammar.
7. **Wayfinding pass on spatial navigation.** For any field-like/room-like navigation concepts, run orientation testing with outsiders to confirm conventional-enough wayfinding survives the spatial metaphor.
8. **Document the dimension-to-label mapping pattern** in stUdio's builder UI so creators understand the hidden four-dimension architecture without that architecture ever becoming visitor-facing.
9. **Define the visual generation layer.** Specify how hOMe derives palette, typography, imagery, graphic motifs, motion, and density from the person's source material; include accessibility and consent constraints.
10. **Design the offer/invitation system.** Create hOMe-native patterns for services, projects, sessions, collaborations, communities, and other invitations so offers feel alive and specific rather than generic Web2 modules or flattened text blocks.
11. **Wire cOMpass and OM Cipher as first-class builder inputs.** Ensure the implementation reads the cOMpass JSON for initial public content and the OM Cipher palette for baseline visual direction before asking the user to manually fill or style the hOMe.
