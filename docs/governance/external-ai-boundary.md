# External AI Boundary and Sacred Mode

Status: v0.1. Canonical governance principle. The full source and original reasoning are preserved verbatim at [`../source-material/governance-principle-v0.1.md`](../source-material/governance-principle-v0.1.md); this doc is the canonical, repo-integrated statement of the principle.

This principle governs **how CommonUnity's AI presences may touch a person's inner record.** It binds **both Nexus and DIGIT** — DIGIT runs on external AI just as Nexus does, so every rule about "external AI" and "boundary-crossing" applies to DIGIT as well.

## The premise

CommonUnity begins from the premise that a human being's inner record is **not ordinary product data**. A person's reflections, OM Cipher, contemplations, emotional material, and symbolic self-patterns are **entrusted material**. They are not simply inputs to be captured, optimised, monetised, or silently accumulated.

Any external AI system — Anthropic, Perplexity, OpenAI, or any future model provider — is treated as **outside the sacred trust boundary** unless there is *both* a signed agreement *and* a verifiable technical control proving otherwise. External AI may be used as a **temporary intelligence processor**, but it must not become the default memory holder of a person's inner life.

## The trust boundary

**Inside** the CommonUnity trust boundary:

- The user's local-first data.
- The Vault or future protected memory layer.
- The OM Cipher source record.
- Private session notes and reflections, unless explicitly offered.
- The mapping between real-world contact identity and pseudonymous Cipher identity.
- The CommonUnity Key — the portable, user-held file carrying the OM Cipher + Living Profile.

**Outside** the trust boundary by default:

- External AI providers.
- General-purpose AI chat interfaces.
- API calls to model providers.
- Support bots, third-party analytics, and opaque vendor systems.
- Any system where retention, review, training use, or access governance cannot be independently verified.

## Nexus (and DIGIT) as a boundary-crossing event

Neither Nexus nor DIGIT is designed as an invisible data sink. **Each meaningful use is a boundary-crossing event: a chosen offering of context into an external intelligence process.**

This does not mean the AI must be weak or shallow. It means its depth is **permissioned**. The system distinguishes between present-moment guidance, session-level context, Vault excerpts, Golden Thread material, and deeper OM Cipher context. **These layers are not silently bundled together.** The user chooses what crosses.

Concretely: minimise what is sent by default; keep real-world identity separate from Cipher identity; avoid sending names, emails, birth data, raw Golden Thread entries, or full OM Cipher records unless explicitly required; provide user-visible controls for deeper context sharing.

## Non-accumulation and the living self

CommonUnity rejects the assumption that more memory is always better. A human being is not a fixed archive. A person changes, evolves, contradicts themselves, outgrows prior self-descriptions, and never steps in the same river twice.

Therefore the system does **not hoard memories** simply because they might become useful. Non-accumulation is a governance principle — and a spiritual discipline. The digital self should remain partial, living, revisable, and releasable.

AI memory carries a special risk: **it can freeze a person inside an older pattern and keep reintroducing that pattern as if it were still true.** Nexus (and DIGIT) should therefore hold memory lightly — able to ask whether a remembered pattern still feels alive, whether a note should be retained, whether a reflection should be released, and whether silence or forgetting is wiser than preservation.

*Memory is sacred because forgetting is also sacred.*

## Private, Secret, Scared, and Sacred

CommonUnity distinguishes privacy from sacredness with care. These words carry related but different energies, and the nuance matters for how a person understands their relationship with the technology.

- **Private** is a boundary of access: *this is not for everyone.* Necessary, but can feel procedural, defensive, or merely technical.
- **Secret** is hidden knowledge. A secret may be protected for good reasons, but may also carry shame, fear, power, or concealment. Not every secret is sacred, and not every sacred thing is secret.
- **Scared** is protection through fear. When a person withholds from fear alone, the nervous system contracts. Fear may be a useful signal, but it is not the foundation of CommonUnity's trust architecture.
- **Sacred** is protected because it has **dignity, depth, timing, and value**. Sacredness does not require religion. It means some thoughts, feelings, prayers, recognitions, and inner movements belong first to the person — to silence, to source, to the body, to the mystery of becoming.

This distinction is **fractal**: it applies to a single note, a Nexus conversation, an OM Cipher, a Vault memory, a community relationship, and the whole CommonUnity field. The question is not only *"is this private?"* but *"what kind of protection does this material require, and from what place is that protection arising?"*

## Sacred Mode

For this reason, the local-only contemplative feature is understood as **Sacred Mode**, not merely Private Mode.

- **Sacred Mode material is never sent to any AI** — not Nexus, not DIGIT, not any external model. It is a place where something can be true without being spoken to AI, saved into a thread, analysed, optimised, or made useful.
- Material may be marked **"not for processing" / "hold only."** Nexus and DIGIT must be able to see that mark and leave the material alone.
- Sacred Mode is a **sacred withdrawal from the senses of the machine** (Pratyahara). It protects *from care, not from paranoia*.

## Data sovereignty through discernment

CommonUnity does not rely on technical controls alone to solve data sovereignty. Technical controls matter, and the system pursues the strongest available: local-first storage, pseudonymous identity, admin minimisation, external-AI minimisation, token masking, retention reduction, and zero-data-retention agreements where available.

But when external providers cannot or will not offer custom retention controls, **sovereignty must also become a practice of discernment.** The answer is not to pretend every risk has been removed; it is to help the person know where to draw the line.

Anything that leaves a person through speech, writing, or digital transmission can be misunderstood, taken out of context, copied, retained, or used in ways not intended. This is not unique to AI — a spoken confidence can be repeated, a journal stolen, a message forwarded. AI makes the issue more powerful because it can process, summarise, pattern, and retain what is offered into it. Therefore CommonUnity teaches that **not everything true needs to be said.** Withholding can be truthful when it arises from care, timing, non-harm, and sacred boundary rather than deception.

The three-gate discipline, with a fourth governance question for this system:

1. Is it true?
2. Is it kind?
3. Is it necessary?
4. **Is this mine to offer into this system?**

The fourth question sits at the centre of Sacred Mode. Some truths become distorted when made useful too quickly.

## Consent, ignorance, and the line

Much of the modern digital economy grew through **unconscious consent** — people clicking yes before understanding what was gathered, inferred, predicted, sold, or used to shape behaviour. Predictive systems became powerful not only because technology advanced, but because people were trained to surrender context without discernment.

CommonUnity must not repeat this pattern in more spiritual language. It should not invite people to hand over their inner lives because the interface feels beautiful or the guidance feels helpful. The deeper purpose is to help people become **more conscious at the threshold where inner life becomes digital signal** — including the recognition that a person's digital trace can become a kind of *clone*: an economic, behavioural, emotional, or symbolic model of the person that may serve systems they do not own.

Even if strong technical data sovereignty becomes possible, the human principle remains: **not everything should be digitised, shared, remembered, or made part of a model.** CommonUnity helps each user discover the line between useful digital reflection and unnecessary self-surrender.

## Eight Limbs as digital governance

The [adapted Eight Limbs](../foundation/adapted-8-limbs.md) serve as the governance frame — a way to ask not only whether a feature *works*, but whether it supports union, coherence, non-harm, and human sovereignty.

- **Yama** — ethical restraint. The system must not harm, deceive, steal attention, steal data, over-consume intimate material, or manipulate toward dependency. Ahimsa applies to architecture, defaults, retention, and the possibility of future misuse.
- **Niyama** — internal discipline. Stay clean, transparent, humble, oriented toward liberation rather than capture. Repeatedly examine motive: is this helping the person become more whole, or increasing attachment, performance, and extraction?
- **Asana** — the steady, easeful seat (*sthira sukham asanam*). If the user is straining, over-prompting, or forcing insight through AI, that is flexing, not union. The AI relationship should not reward force; it should help create a stable seat for reflection.
- **Pranayama** — rhythm, breath, pause. Respect intervals. Not every reflection requires an immediate AI response. The AI should not assume the user resumes as the exact same person who stopped.
- **Pratyahara** — withdrawal of the senses. This is what makes **Sacred Mode** essential: a place for thoughts, contemplations, and prayers that are shared with no one, including AI.
- **Dharana** — concentration. The AI protects focus, reduces distraction, clarifies the object of attention — becoming a guardian of attention rather than an interpreter constantly pulling the user outward.
- **Dhyana** — sustained flow. Once trust is established and the user's projects and relationships are formed in stUdio, the AI helps maintain coherence around the work so the human can remain in deeper continuity. Technology should reduce fragmentation, not add noise.
- **Samadhi** — union. AI does not claim, interpret, or occupy this space. It serves the conditions around it — knowing when to speak, when to hold structure, and when to step back. The correct promise of technology is to ease human suffering, not increase it.

The milestone integrity audit ([`./audit-rituals.md`](./audit-rituals.md)) is where features are walked against these limbs.

## Practical design commitments

- Minimise what is sent to external AI by default.
- Keep real-world identity separate from OM Cipher identity.
- Keep contact information separate from reflective and symbolic data.
- Avoid sending names, emails, birth data, raw Golden Thread entries, or full OM Cipher records unless explicitly required.
- Provide user-visible controls for deeper context sharing.
- Preserve local-first or user-controlled storage as the canonical source of truth.
- Treat provider policies as useful but insufficient without contractual and technical safeguards.
- Prefer zero-data-retention agreements wherever external AI processes sensitive reflective material.
- Avoid hoarding memory by default; make release, forgetting, review, and revision part of the memory architecture.
- Provide a true Sacred Mode for local-only contemplation that is not shared with AI.
- Design Nexus and DIGIT to support rhythm, pause, attention, and coherence rather than constant output.
- Treat higher stages of human development as places where technology serves the surrounding field rather than claiming the inner experience.

## Beta focus area

During the beta period, this principle is an **active area of inquiry**, not a hidden legal policy. Beta users help form the governance culture by testing the lived experience of conscious data offering:

- What does it feel like to share inner material with an AI?
- What kinds of context make the AI more helpful — and what feels too private, too powerful, or unnecessary?
- When does AI support reflection, and when does it begin to feel extractive?
- What should remain local, protected, or unshared? What does a minimum viable digital self require?
- What should be remembered, what released, and what never offered into AI?
- Where is the line between useful digital reflection and unnecessary self-surrender?
- What can be spoken to Nexus/DIGIT, what should remain in Sacred Mode, and what should not be digitised at all?

## Foundational position

CommonUnity does **not** promise that external AI is inherently safe. It promises that the crossing into external AI is made **visible, limited, intentional, and consented.** The aim is not to reject intelligence tools, but to use them without surrendering the dignity, sovereignty, and sacredness of the human record.

The deeper question CommonUnity holds:

> How digital can I become without losing sovereignty over myself?

The answer is never fixed by the system. It is discovered through conscious practice, trustworthy architecture, and living relationship.
